学习背景

这属于System Design：根据用户需求设计多个组件如何协作。先说明假设，再讨论容量、数据、失败和取舍。

Question

Design a rate limiter for an expensive inference API.

请设计 Rate Limiting and Capacity 场景中的系统，说明需求、架构、取舍和失败处理。

Key Terms

Rate Limiting and Capacity
分别限制到达速率和正在执行的请求数。
Requirement
系统必须满足的行为或性能约束。
Trade-off
一个选择带来的收益及付出的代价。
Failure mode
依赖或组件出错时的具体表现。

Interview Prompt

Design a rate limiter for an expensive inference API.

Clarifying Questions

Is the quota per user, tenant or provider? Are bursts allowed? What happens when the shared store fails?

Functional and Non-Functional Requirements

Enforce tenant quotas and bound in-flight requests. Assume 20 requests/sec and 3 seconds mean service time, implying about 60 concurrent requests before headroom.

The numerical targets here are practice assumptions to negotiate with an interviewer, not measured project results.

Architecture and Data Flow

Design Decisions and Alternatives

Use token buckets for rate and semaphores for concurrency. Return a documented retry response when the budget is exhausted; cap queue wait by deadline.

A global atomic counter is precise but adds a dependency. Local partitioned budgets trade exactness for availability.

Failure Scenarios

Provider slows down: concurrency grows even if arrival rate is unchanged. Shed load and reduce retries before the queue becomes unbounded.

Step-by-step interview script
  1. I would first clarify: Is the quota per user, tenant or provider? Are bursts allowed? What happens when the shared store fails?
  2. I would propose this contract: Enforce tenant quotas and bound in-flight requests. Assume 20 requests/sec and 3 seconds mean service time, implying about 60 concurrent requests before headroom.
  3. The main data flow is Client → Auth → Admission Limiter → Bounded Queue → Model Provider.
  4. The key decision is: Use token buckets for rate and semaphores for concurrency. Return a documented retry response when the budget is exhausted; cap queue wait by deadline.
  5. The trade-off is: A global atomic counter is precise but adds a dependency. Local partitioned budgets trade exactness for availability.
  6. I would test this failure: Provider slows down: concurrency grows even if arrival rate is unchanged. Shed load and reduce retries before the queue becomes unbounded.
  7. I would close by checking the design against the requirements and identifying the next measurement or implementation risk.

Follow-up Questions

Use Little law with units and distinguish steady-state estimates from burst capacity.

How would you adapt the design if traffic increased tenfold?

I would identify the saturated resource from measured utilization and queueing, then consider partitioning, caching or additional workers while preserving ownership, idempotency and data consistency. I would not assume that multiplying replicas fixes a serialized dependency.

Follow-up: Which component in your drawing is the first bottleneck?

Practice and Self-Review

First learning day: 15 minutes explanation, 20 minutes drawing, 10 minutes follow-ups. Paired practice day: 30 minutes independent design and spoken explanation, 15 minutes comparing requirements, data flow, failure handling and trade-offs with this case. On buffer days change one constraint.
