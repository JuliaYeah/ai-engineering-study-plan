Interview Prompt

Design a contract-review assistant for a new business domain.

Clarifying Questions

Who reviews legal interpretations? Which clauses and jurisdictions matter? How are document permissions represented?

Functional and Non-Functional Requirements

Extract cited clauses, flag uncertainty and route decisions to a human. Do not autonomously make binding legal decisions.

The numerical targets here are practice assumptions to negotiate with an interviewer, not measured project results.

Architecture and Data Flow

Design Decisions and Alternatives

Reuse retrieval, evaluation and approval patterns, but create domain-specific labels and failure criteria. Preserve source spans and model/prompt versions.

A general chat interface is flexible; structured findings and review queues make evidence and ownership easier to audit.

Failure Scenarios

OCR drops a negation or an injected clause redirects the model: compare source text, test adversarial inputs and require reviewer confirmation.

AI Decision Checklist

  • Model: start with a measured task baseline; select by quality, latency, cost and permitted data handling.
  • Data and context: preserve source/version/permission scope; validate generated tool arguments outside the model.
  • Evaluation: use representative development and held-out cases, deterministic checks for numbers, human-calibrated review for open text.
  • Failures and oversight: bound calls and spend, distinguish failure from success, require real authorization for consequential actions.
  • Cost and latency: record tokens, retries, queue wait and critical-path stages; re-evaluate quality after optimization.
Step-by-step interview script
  1. I would first clarify: Who reviews legal interpretations? Which clauses and jurisdictions matter? How are document permissions represented?
  2. I would propose this contract: Extract cited clauses, flag uncertainty and route decisions to a human. Do not autonomously make binding legal decisions.
  3. The main data flow is Upload → Authorized Parsing → Clause Retrieval → Structured Findings → Human Review → Audit.
  4. The key decision is: Reuse retrieval, evaluation and approval patterns, but create domain-specific labels and failure criteria. Preserve source spans and model/prompt versions.
  5. The trade-off is: A general chat interface is flexible; structured findings and review queues make evidence and ownership easier to audit.
  6. I would test this failure: OCR drops a negation or an injected clause redirects the model: compare source text, test adversarial inputs and require reviewer confirmation.
  7. I would close by checking the design against the requirements and identifying the next measurement or implementation risk.

Follow-up Questions

Explain what transfers from financial RAG and which evaluation assumptions must be rebuilt.

How would you adapt the design if traffic increased tenfold?

I would identify the saturated resource from measured utilization and queueing, then consider partitioning, caching or additional workers while preserving ownership, idempotency and data consistency. I would not assume that multiplying replicas fixes a serialized dependency.

Follow-up: Which component in your drawing is the first bottleneck?

Practice and Self-Review

First learning day: 15 minutes explanation, 20 minutes drawing, 10 minutes follow-ups. Paired practice day: 30 minutes independent design and spoken explanation, 15 minutes comparing requirements, data flow, failure handling and trade-offs with this case. On buffer days change one constraint.
